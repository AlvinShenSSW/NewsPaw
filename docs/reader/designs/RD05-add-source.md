## Spec review

After RD03 the owner can manage categories; after RD04 the package can collect one stored feed. Nothing yet puts a source into a library. This issue adds the three things that do: the Add source sheet (address → detection → category → preview → save), the source rows under each category in Library with their state, and the Source settings sheet.

Core need: a pasted address ends, without surprises, in exactly one of five visible outcomes — a saved feed source with its first articles, a saved web-page source, a refusal that says where the address already is, a failure with its reason, or nothing at all (cancel) — and what the owner sees in Library afterwards is true.

The mock-up boards `AddSource`, `Library` and `IPad` of the design canvas were read (2026-11-11). They add four facts ux.md does not state: each picker row has a second line "RSS · latest item 25 minutes ago", so every declared feed is fetched; a category row has a leading disclosure chevron and no trailing one; a failing source row has a second line with the status sentence and a warning mark in place of the count; the iPad sidebar has "Add source" at its bottom.

**Settled here**, because the issue and the specification leave it open:

1. **What "the same address" is.** Two addresses are the same when their normal forms are equal as strings. The normal form: surrounding white space removed; `https://` put in front when the text does not begin with a scheme — a letter, then letters, digits, `+`, `-` or `.`, then `://`, at the very start; a `://` further in, as in a query, does not count; scheme and host in lower case; a default port (80 for http, 443 for https) removed; an empty path written `/`; the fragment removed. Nothing else changes: the path's case and trailing slash, the query and its order, `www.`, tracking parameters, percent-escapes and dot segments stay as typed, and http and https are different addresses. Reason: each of the excluded rewrites can change which document a server returns or is a guess, a wrong "duplicate" blocks a legitimate source with no way round it, and a missed duplicate costs only a second row the owner can delete. Addresses that are not http or https, carry a user name or password (RD04 refuses them), have no host, or are longer than 2,048 characters are refused before any request.
2. **Where the normal form lives.** In `source.url`, which RD02 already made unique. What the owner typed is not kept; Source settings shows and copies the normal form. No column and no migration are added. Every writer of `source.url` must normalise first — RD08 and RD20 are told so in the handoff notes.
3. **One scheme upgrade, at add time only.** When the fetch of an http address ends at its https twin — the final address's normal form equals the requested normal form with the scheme changed to `https` — the https form is what is stored. Reason: it removes a plain-text hop from every later collection and makes the common "typed http, site is https" case meet the duplicate rule. After an upgrade the upgraded address is looked up in the library once more, so a duplicate reached this way is refused before Save: the typed address with the usual message and where it is filed, a declared feed by marking it "Already in ⟨category⟩". No other redirect target is ever stored (RD04: a stored address is never rewritten).
4. **Discovery reads `<link>` elements only.** A `<link>` in the examined part of the document whose `rel` tokens include `alternate` and not `stylesheet`, whose `type` (parameters dropped, case ignored) is `application/rss+xml`, `application/atom+xml`, `application/rdf+xml` or `application/feed+json`, and whose `href`, trimmed of white space, resolves — against a valid http(s) `<base href>`, else the fetch's final address; the rule is written out by hand, not left to a library's resolver — to an address the normal form accepts. `application/json` is not read: on most sites it is the WordPress API link, and each candidate costs a request; a JSON feed declared that way is added by pasting its own address, and the specification says so. No `<a>` scanning and no guessing of `/feed` or `/rss.xml`: those are requests to addresses neither the owner nor the page named. Candidates are de-duplicated by normal form, a candidate equal to the page's own requested or final normal form is dropped, they are kept in document order and capped at 5; when the page declares more, the sheet says how many and that the first 5 are shown.
5. **Discovery uses the document RD04's fetcher returned, and builds no tree from it.** The page is requested once. Only its first 1 MB is examined, in one linear scan that tokenises every tag and keeps `<link>`, `<base>`, `<title>` and `<meta>`; its exact rules are in Approach. Attributes are read from the matched tags only. SwiftSoup may be given the text of one matched tag, never the document: its tree builder is quadratic on crafted markup (measured: 200 KB of nested table cells take 19 seconds, 1 MB of an ordinary page 0.06), and RD04 was hardened the same way in its version 5. The scan runs when the media type is `text/html` or `application/xhtml+xml`, or when the first 1,024 bytes contain `<html` or `<!doctype html` in any letter case.
6. **Every candidate is fetched once, before the picker is shown, inside one time limit.** At most 4 at a time, each through RD04's fetcher under RD04's limits, and the whole candidate phase under one overall limit of 20 seconds. A candidate that has not answered by then is listed as "could not check" and can still be chosen. Choosing it returns to the address step with that address filled in and runs an ordinary detection: the progress line, then that detection's ordinary outcome — feed, page, duplicate or failure. The earlier candidate list and "This page itself" are gone, and the name is reset to what the new detection suggests. The limit ends unanswered fetches as "not checked" inside the call; it is not the call's cancellation, and the detection still returns its result. A candidate already in this library is marked and not fetched. So the picker shows the mock-up's line for every row it could check and never presents a failed feed as ready. The detection is one cancellable call whose worst case is 40 seconds — 20 for the page, 20 for the candidates — with a progress line and Cancel on screen throughout. Parsed candidates are held only until the sheet closes.
7. **Saving reuses the preview's fetch; there is no second request.** Save writes the source row, the articles of the feed already parsed for the preview, the validators and the success time in one transaction. This is how N03's "nothing is saved on failure" and the issue's "Save collects the source once" are both kept: every network failure happens before Save, and Save can fail only on storage or on the duplicate rule, in which case neither the source nor any article exists.
8. **A page that declares feeds can still be saved as a web page.** When the address is an HTML page, the choice list ends with a row "This page itself". N03's table gives a web-page source "any address" as input, and the owner's own example of a web-page source (spec section 0) is a site that also has a feed. ux S6 only offers the web-page route when no feed is found; ux.md gets a sentence. Decided by the driver under the owner's delegation ("For the owner", below).
9. **"Save as a web page" is also offered when the server answered but the app's fetch could not use the answer** (`http:<status>`, `too_large`, `too_many_redirects`). RD04's requests carry a generic user agent and no cookies, and sites that refuse such clients are exactly the ones a web-page source exists for; without this they could never be added, from here or from RD10's "Save this page as a source". Not offered for `unreachable`, `timed_out`, `gone` or `unsupported_address`. Nothing is saved unless the owner chooses it. ux.md gets a sentence. Decided by the driver under the owner's delegation ("For the owner").
10. **The suggested list is Swift constants in the package, eleven entries**, each a feed that `docs/archive/source-strategy.md` records as answered by a manual probe on 2026-11-07 UTC (table in Approach). A library whose partition is not normal is given an empty list by the package, so no interface can show it in the adult partition by mistake. Choosing an entry runs the ordinary flow with that address: detection, preview, category, Save. None ships unverified: before the pull request is ready the implementer checks each entry once, by hand, through the app in the simulator against the real feed, and an entry that does not fetch, parse and show a sensible preview is removed — from the constants, their test and the table — in the same pull request.
11. **Site icon.** A monogram tile (the first character of the source's name), as the mock-up draws it; a globe for a web-page source. Nothing fetches a site's icon: no issue plans it, and it is listed under Non-goals.
12. **Unread counts.** RD05 shows the count on each feed-like source row, because issue #126 says so and its own criterion ("a globe and no count") presupposes it. The counts on category rows and on All Unread are RD09's, as issue #130 says in so many words. Until RD09 nothing can mark an article read, so a source's count is the number of its stored articles.
13. **RD03's empty destinations stop being true once a source is saved.** "Nothing new today", "All caught up" and "No articles in this category" are replaced, only while the destination's scope holds unread articles, by one sentence, the same in all three: "⟨N⟩ unread articles — article lists are not available in this version yet." It is one catalog key; the handoff notes name exactly what RD09 deletes.
14. **What a tap on a source row does.** It opens Source settings — the only screen a source has before RD09 (the list) and RD10 (the browser), which re-point the tap. The long-press menu offers Source settings, Pause / Resume and Delete; Refresh is RD06's.
15. **Sources and edit mode.** A source is moved from its settings (the Category row), not by dragging; S2's "edit mode: move sources between categories" is met by that other route and ux.md gets a sentence (decided by the driver under the owner's delegation; "For the owner"). Edit mode leaves expansion as it is. Source rows carry `.deleteDisabled(true)` and `.moveDisabled(true)`: they have no swipe-to-delete, no delete control and no reorder handle, and a source is deleted only from its menu or its settings. Both points were measured with RD03's list shape — without the guards a swipe on a source row runs the category deletion at the source's index within its own category, and collapsing on entering edit mode (version 2's answer) strips the edit controls from every category but the first. Expansion is kept in memory, collapsed at launch; saving a source expands its category.
16. **Source settings is a draft with Cancel and Save**, like RD03's Category settings: name, category, paused and the options are written together, only the columns that changed. Copy, Update Now and Delete act at once.
17. **Options that nothing acts on yet are labelled.** "Translate titles", "Body" and, for a web page, "Translate when opened" are stored now and used by RD14, RD11/RD12 and RD16. Each group carries a one-line footnote saying so; the issue that makes an option work removes its footnote.
18. **The status section has "Update Now".** ux section 4 requires a next step after every failure message, and without it a failing status could not arise or be cleared before RD06. It collects that one source with RD04's collector. It is not shown for a paused or web-page source: RD04's collector does not look at `paused` — not collecting a paused source is the caller's rule, and here this is the caller. Scheduling, the cap across sources, pull to refresh and the menu's Refresh stay with RD06.
19. **Copy uses the ordinary pasteboard.** N09 says sharing the owner starts is not restricted, and no requirement asks for more. (Versions 1 and 2 wrote the address local-only; the debate removed that.)
20. **App Transport Security.** One key, `NSAllowsArbitraryLoads`, in a partial Info.plist merged with the generated one (Approach).
21. **"Paste" is the system's paste button with a neutral fill.** It is the only control that works: measured in round 2, a plain button that reads the pasteboard gets nothing on a fresh install — no text and no prompt — while the system button pastes text without a prompt. Its fill is one new palette token used for nothing else, a fixed dark grey, `#5E5D58` in light and in dark, on which the label the system draws is white at 6.6:1 (measured in both appearances); the accent is not used as a fill (RD03 criterion 9). The button is 34 pt high at the default text size and cannot be padded to a larger target, so it is the one exception to the 44 pt rule of ux section 6, listed under "For the owner". The field's own edit menu pastes as well.
22. **No partition label in the category picker yet.** ux S6 says the picker "shows which partition it is in". Before RD18 there is one partition and RD03's invariant forbids hinting at another; RD18 adds the label.
23. **The mock-up's footnote "titles will be translated by the normal partition's model" is not shown.** No model exists before RD13/RD14; RD14 adds it.
24. **A category created inside the sheet stays.** "New Category…" in the sheet's category picker writes that category at once, exactly as "+" › New Category does, and Cancel does not take it back. "Nothing is saved" before Save and on failure is about sources and articles.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Normal form.** A table-driven package test covers every rule of decision 1 — each rewrite, each thing deliberately not rewritten, each refusal, and a scheme recognised only at the start of the text (`example.com/go?to=http://other.example` gets `https://` in front) — and that the normal form of a normal form is itself.
2. **A feed address.** For RSS 2.0, RSS 1.0, Atom and JSON Feed fixtures, detection returns one feed candidate with its format, title, site link and its latest three items with their times. Saving it leaves one source of kind `feed` and its articles; the transport saw exactly one request.
3. **A page with feeds.** Detection returns the declared feeds under the rules of decisions 4–6: `href` trimmed; relative addresses and `<base href>` resolved; `rel` and `type` matched as specified; `application/json`, `rel="alternate stylesheet"`, language alternates and unusable `href`s ignored; a `<link>` written inside a comment, a script, a style element or another tag's attribute value ignored; a `<base>` that stands after the links still applied; character references in `href` decoded; duplicates and a link to the page itself dropped; more than 5 capped with the declared total reported. The page was requested once, each fetched candidate once, never more than 4 at a time, and nothing else the page mentions (scripts, images, anchors, icons) was requested. The candidate phase ends at its overall limit (injected shorter in the test): a candidate still unanswered is in the result as "could not check" while the call returns normally with the others; in the sheet it can be chosen, which returns to the address step with its address filled in and runs an ordinary detection whose outcome replaces the earlier list and resets the name. A candidate that fails is in the result with its reason and cannot be chosen; the others can. Choosing one and saving stores that feed's address, its articles, and the page as its site link when the feed names none. Discovery time grows in proportion to the document's size, not faster: for each of two hostile shapes — deeply nested table cells, and `<link` openings that never close — a test scans a size N and a size 4N and requires the larger to take less than 8 times the smaller, best of three runs, as RD04's criterion 13 does.
4. **A page without feeds, and a page saved as itself.** A page with no usable declaration yields "No feed was found at this address" with "Save as a Web Page" and "Try Another Address". A page with feeds offers "This page itself" as the last choice. Either way the saved source has kind `webpage`, the page's title as its name (the host when there is no title; a Shift_JIS page's title reads correctly), no articles, and appears in Library with a globe and no count. No request was made beyond the first.
5. **Duplicates.** An address whose normal form is already stored in this library is refused before any request is made; the message names the category and the source's name and offers "Show in Library". A declared feed that is already stored is marked "Already in ⟨category⟩", is not fetched and cannot be chosen. An address that turns out to be stored only after the https upgrade is treated the same way once its fetch has shown that: the typed address is answered as a duplicate with where it is filed, a declared feed is marked. If the address is taken between detection and Save, Save fails with the duplicate error and writes nothing. "Show in Library" closes the sheet and shows that category expanded: at the Library tab's root in the tab structure; in the sidebar pane from 1100 pt; in the slide-over, opened, below 1100 pt.
6. **The other partition.** This is structural: a library holds only its own connection pool, and no function added here takes a partition, a store or a second library — a review check. Package tests show the behaviour: with both libraries open and the adult one holding an address, detecting and saving that address in the normal library succeeds, and nothing in the result or the error path differs from adding a new address; the reverse; and the same case with the adult library's file holding the address but never opened by the store. The app cannot reach the adult library before RD18, so this criterion has no interface check.
7. **Failures.** For each RD04 failure code on the typed address the sheet shows "Could not read this address · ⟨reason⟩" and "Try Again", which repeats the detection; `http:<status>`, `too_large` and `too_many_redirects` also offer "Save as a Web Page". When the app put `https://` in front and the result is `unreachable` or `timed_out`, one more sentence says to type `http://` in front if the site only supports http. After any failure, and after Cancel at any moment including mid-detection, the library holds exactly the sources and articles it held before. A category the owner created inside the sheet stays (decision 24).
8. **Saving is atomic.** The source, its first articles, the validators and the success time are written in one transaction. Tests: a save into an unknown category and a save of an address already stored write nothing; and with a SQLite trigger installed on the test library that aborts an article insert, the save fails and leaves neither the source row nor any article.
9. **Suggested sources.** The list is the entries of the table below that passed the manual check — eleven at most — each in normal form, https, unique, with a name and a group; a test pins the exact shipped set. Every shipped entry has a line in the pull request with the date and the result of fetching, parsing and previewing it once through the app in the simulator; an entry without such a line, or with a failed one, is not in the list. A normal library offers them; an adult library is given none. In the sheet, entries already in the library are marked "Added"; choosing another one runs detection on its address.
10. **Source rows.** Under each category, when expanded: monogram or globe, name, unread count (feed-like, when above zero), a pause mark and dimming when paused, a warning mark and the status sentence when failing. State is carried by a glyph and a VoiceOver value, not by colour or dimming alone. The disclosure control is its own 44 × 44 pt target with a label; a tap on the rest of a category row still opens the category. A swipe on a source row offers no Delete, and in edit mode a source row has neither a delete control nor a reorder handle. With categories expanded, edit mode shows a reorder handle and a delete control for every category, a drag reorders categories, and every criterion of RD03 about categories — create, rename, icon, reorder, delete with sources — still passes with sources present.
11. **Source settings, feed-like.** Name (the same kind of rule as a category: trimmed, 1–200 characters, message under the field), address (read-only, Copy), category, paused, translate titles (follow the global setting / on / off), body (automatic / always fetch the page / feed content only), status, delete. Save writes only what changed and nothing in the status columns; every value is still there after a relaunch. On failure the alert is attached to the sheet and the sheet stays open with its values.
12. **Source settings, web page.** Only name, address with Copy, category, "Translate when opened" (off by default, stored in `translate_page`) and delete. No status section.
13. **Status.** The section shows exactly one of: working with the last success time; could not update with the reason and, when there was one, the last success time; paused; not updated yet — and the time of the newest item when the source has articles. The sentences and the nine reasons are the ones in the wording table, in English and Simplified Chinese; a code the app does not know reads as the generic reason. "Update Now" collects the source once, shows "Updating…" meanwhile, and the section then shows the stored result. A UI test drives working → failing → working with the fixture transport.
14. **Moving.** Changing a source's category changes `category_id` and `updated_at` and nothing else: its articles, their identifiers, read and starred times, and its status columns are byte-for-byte the same. Only categories of the same library can be chosen.
15. **Deleting.** The confirmation names the source and says how many articles are deleted with it, starred ones included. Afterwards the source, its articles, bodies and seen identifiers are gone and every other source is untouched. A settings sheet open on a deleted source closes.
16. **Plain http.** The built Info.plist (Debug and Release) contains `NSAppTransportSecurity` with `NSAllowsArbitraryLoads` true, still contains every generated key it had before, and still has no scene manifest. In the simulator, by hand: an `http://` feed address is added successfully, and the same build without the key fails on it (shown once).
17. **Truthful destinations.** With no unread article in scope, Today, All Unread and a category say what they said after RD03. With some, each says in one sentence how many there are and that article lists are not available in this version yet.
18. **Structure.** Add source and Source settings are presented from the root through the same single sheet state as the category sheets; what is typed, the detection in flight and its result live in a model, so crossing 700 pt loses nothing. Alerts and dialogs raised while a sheet is open are attached to the sheet. Every new string is in the catalog with Simplified Chinese and the strings check passes. Every new icon-only control has a label and measures at least 44 × 44 pt (the system paste button is the one exception to the size, criterion 20); at the largest accessibility text size source rows grow and nothing is clipped.
19. **No test reaches a real host.** Package tests use a fake transport. Under a UI-test library directory the app's transport is the debug-only fixture transport. It answers a fixed table — `example.invalid` addresses, and one suggested address answered with a synthetic feed, so that the suggested-list test never reaches the real host — and reports every other address as unreachable; it is absent from release builds.
20. **Paste.** The Paste control is the system paste button with the neutral fill. A UI test sets the pasteboard, taps the button and finds that text in the address field, with no prompt; it records the button's measured height in the evidence rather than requiring 44 pt. The palette test covers a white label on the neutral fill at 4.5:1 or more in light and dark.
21. **Evidence.** Package tests; UI tests on the three simulators; the manual simulator record of the Test plan, each line with its date and result and marked as checked by hand against a real host; screenshots of the sheet's five outcomes, an expanded category with a working, a paused, a failing and a web-page source, and both settings variants, one in dark appearance, one in Simplified Chinese, one at the largest text size.

## Frozen issue contract

**In.** The twenty-one criteria.

**Invariants.**
- Detection never writes. Until Save, a library is only read (the duplicate lookups); the one write the sheet can make before Save is a category the owner creates in it (decision 24).
- Every function added to `Library` works on that library alone. Nothing takes a `Partition`, a `LibraryStore` or a second `Library`; nothing reads `Library.partition` except to hand an adult library an empty suggested list. The adult functions stay `internal`.
- RD02's invariants hold: identifiers enter SQL as lowercase text through the one helper; no `CHECK`; migrations append-only — and this issue adds none. An update writes only the columns it changes, so a collection in flight and an edit never overwrite each other (RD04 criterion 14).
- `source.url` only ever receives a normal form.
- Everything fetched is data (N12). A page is read for `<link>`, `<base>`, `<title>` and `<meta charset>` and nothing else, by a linear scan that builds no tree from the document; nothing in it is executed, and the only addresses requested because of it are its declared feeds, at most 5, through RD04's transport with RD04's limits. Titles from feeds and pages are shown as plain text and cut to 200 characters where they become a name.
- The app makes requests only through the one transport handed to its models; under a UI-test library directory that transport is never the real one.
- RD03's invariants hold: storage only through `Library`'s public API; no `@testable import` in the app; system components for bars, lists and sheets; palette tokens only, no control filled with the accent unless its label is the on-accent token; debug seams inside `#if DEBUG`; no key built at run time.
- No dependency is added or changed.

**Allowed user-visible change.** The Add source sheet and its entry points (Library's "+" menu, the sidebar's bottom section); source rows and the disclosure control in the category list, with the trailing chevron of category rows removed; the Source settings sheet; the three interim destination texts; plain-http addresses now load.

**For the owner.** Seven things here go beyond what ux.md and the specification say. The driver decided them under the owner's delegation (spec section 11), and the pull request repeats this paragraph. (1) Add source offers "This page itself", so a page that declares feeds can still be saved as a web page — extends ux S6. (2) When a site answered but refused or overran the app's own fetch, the failure also offers "Save as a Web Page" — extends ux S6. (3) A source is moved to another category from its settings, not by dragging in edit mode — ux S2. (4) Looking for feeds lets a page make the app request up to five addresses that the page declares, under the same transport rules as any feed: http or https only, no credentials, RD04's limits — N12. (5) Source settings has "Update Now" in its status section — beyond ux S13. (6) A source's row shows a monogram tile where ux S2 says "site icon"; nothing fetches site icons. (7) The Paste button is the system's own control, which is 34 pt high at the default text size and cannot be made taller — an exception, for this one control, to the 44 pt targets of ux section 6; it is used because it is the only way to paste that works and it asks for no permission.

**Non-goals.** GitHub and Hugging Face recognition (RD07, RD26) — such an address is treated like any page; OPML and configuration import and their rows in the sheet (RD08, RD20); opening a web-page source or a feed source's list (RD10, RD09); category and total unread counts (RD09); refresh on entry, pull to refresh, the menu's Refresh, the request cap across sources, retention (RD06); fetching site icons, which no issue plans (rows show a monogram tile); the partition label and anything adult in the interface (RD18); acting on the translate and body options (RD11, RD12, RD14, RD16); first run (RD21); `feed:` addresses; trying http when https fails; drag and drop of sources; swipe-to-delete on a source row; remembering expansion across launches; sorting sources other than by name.

**Causal boundary.**
- `ios/NewsPawKit/Sources/NewsPawKit/Feeds/` — new: `SourceAddress`, `FeedDiscovery`, `SourceDetector`, `SuggestedSources`, `FixtureTransport` (all of it `#if DEBUG`). Nothing in RD04's files changes except the one refactoring named in the next line.
- `Model/Source.swift` (public), a new `Model/SourceSummary.swift` (summary, status); a new `Store/Library+Sources.swift`; `Store/Library+Collection.swift` only to make RD04's storing step callable inside another transaction, behaviour unchanged; `Store/LibraryError.swift` (new cases); `Model/Palette.swift` and its test, for the one neutral-fill token of decision 21.
- `ios/NewsPawKit/Tests/NewsPawKitTests/Feeds/**`, `Store/**`, `Fixtures/pages/**`.
- `ios/NewsPaw/**`; `ios/NewsPawUITests/**`; `ios/NewsPaw-Info.plist` (new); `ios/NewsPaw.xcodeproj/project.pbxproj` for one build setting in the app target's two configurations.
- `docs/reader/ux.md` — one sentence each: S2, sources are moved from Source settings (decision 15); S6, a page that declares feeds can also be saved as itself (decision 8); S6, a failure in which the site answered also offers the web-page route (decision 9). `docs/reader/spec.md` — one sentence each: N02, what the same address is (decision 1); N03, which declarations discovery reads, and that a JSON feed declared as `application/json` is added by pasting its own address; N12, feed discovery requests the feeds a page declares, at most five, under the collection rules; section 10, when and how the suggested feeds were checked. `AGENTS.md` ("Checks": UI tests answer from fixtures).

## Approach

**Package: the address.** `SourceAddress` is a pure function from text to a normal form or one of five problems (empty, not http or https, has credentials, malformed, too long), plus whether `https://` was put in front, and the function that applies decision 3 to a requested address and the final address of its fetch.

**Package: discovery.** `FeedDiscovery` is pure: bytes, media type, charset, and the requested and final addresses in; the page's title, its declared feeds (address, declared title, declared type) and the number declared before the cap, out. It takes the first 1 MB and decodes it lossily — `NSString.stringEncoding(for:encodingOptions:convertedString:usedLossyConversion:)` with one suggested encoding, only that one, lossy conversion allowed — as the charset of the HTTP header, else the one a `<meta>` in the first 1,024 bytes declares, else UTF-8, so that a bad byte or a character cut at the edge never loses the text. Then the one linear scan of decision 5, which never backs up. Its rules, each with its own test case:
- It tokenises every tag, aware of quotes: a tag ends at the first `>` outside a quoted attribute value, and text inside another tag's attribute value is never read as a tag. A tag with no end within 8,192 characters is skipped.
- A tag name ends at white space, `/` or `>` and is compared ignoring case, so `basefont` and `linker` do not match.
- Comments and the contents of `script`, `style` and `textarea` are passed over as text.
- Character references in an `href` are decoded (`&amp;`, `&#038;`, `&#x26;`).
- Raw `href`s are collected during the scan and resolved after it ends, against the first `<base>` that has a usable `href`, wherever in the document it stands.
- The charset is read from `<meta charset>` and from `<meta http-equiv="Content-Type" content="…charset=…">`.
- The title is the text of the first `<title>` up to its end tag, cut to 1,000 characters before its entities are decoded.
- Accepted approximations: a `<!…>` that is not a comment ends at the first `>`; `<!-->` is a complete comment; the contents of `textarea` are treated as text.

**Package: detection.** `SourceDetector` holds a transport and has one entry point: text and a `Library` in, a `Detection` out. It throws only on cancellation or a storage error. In order: normal form (or `invalidAddress`); a lookup of that address in the library (or `duplicate`, with the source's name and category — no request made); one fetch through RD04's fetcher; decision 3's upgrade and, when it applied, a second lookup of the upgraded address (or `duplicate`); then
- a feed → `feed`, one candidate;
- not a feed, and an HTML document → `page`, with its title and its candidates, each fetched as decision 6 says and each ending as usable, already in the library (looked up before its fetch and again after an upgrade), unusable with a reason, or not checked within the limit;
- not a feed and not HTML → `page` with no candidates and no title;
- any other failure → `failed` with the reason, whether "Save as a Web Page" applies, and whether the app put `https://` in front.

A usable candidate carries what the sheet shows — the address to store (the requested normal form, upgraded as decision 3 says when its own fetch was redirected to its https twin), format, title, site link, the latest three items (by published time, newest first; undated items after dated ones in feed order), the newest time, the item count — and, not public, the parsed feed and validators that Save will store.

**Package: sources on `Library`.** Public, each in one transaction:
- `addFeedSource(candidate, name:, categoryID:)` — checks the category, inserts the source (kind `feed`, defaults from the schema), stores the candidate's parsed feed with RD04's storing step under one clock reading. Throws `unknownCategory`, `duplicateAddress`, the name errors.
- `addWebPageSource(address:, name:, categoryID:)` — normalises the address again itself; kind `webpage`.
- `existingSource(atAddress:)` — the name and category of the source at a normal form, for the duplicate message.
- `sourceSummaries()` — every source with its unread count, newest item time and derived status, in one read; ordered in Swift by name (localised, case-insensitive), then identifier.
- `updateSource(id, changes)` — name, category, paused, translate titles, body mode, translate when opened; writes the columns that differ and `updated_at`; refuses an option that does not belong to the source's kind.
- `deleteSource(id)`.
- `suggestedSources` — the list for a normal library, empty otherwise.
- `sourceNameProblem(_:)` — the public name rule, as RD03 did for categories.

`Source` and its three enums become public for reading. The status is a pure function of a source's columns: paused; else could not update (reason, last success) when `last_error` is set; else working (last success); else not updated yet. A web-page source has none. The reason comes from RD04's tolerant reader, made public: the eight codes (the deadline has its own, `timed_out`), with `http:` carrying its number, and "unknown" for anything else.

## Key implementation notes

- Read the merged RD03 and RD04 code before anything else and map this design's roles onto the real names. RD04's design already gives the parsed feed its format, the non-feed answer its charset and the deadline its own code; if the merged code lacks one of them, stop and report rather than adding it here.
- Order: package first (address, discovery, detector, sources on `Library`, suggested list), then the Info.plist with its `plutil` check, then the category list with disclosure and source rows — and prove with RD03's own UI tests that reorder, delete and selection still work — before any sheet is built. If the list cannot be made to work as criterion 10 says, stop and report.
- Make RD04's storing step a function of a `Database` so that the add transaction can call it; RD04's collector keeps calling it inside its own transaction. Do not copy it.
- The duplicate check that counts is the unique constraint inside the add transaction. The lookups before it only save requests and word the message.
- A candidate found on the page is checked against the library before it is fetched; the typed address before anything is fetched.
- `updateSource` builds its `UPDATE` from the changed fields only. Never write a whole `Source` record back: that would overwrite the status columns a collection may be writing.
- Sort sources in Swift; SQLite's `NOCASE` is ASCII-only. The summary query aggregates and must not select `content_html`.
- Discovery never throws on bad input: a page that cannot be parsed has no feeds and no title.
- Discovery builds no tree. Never hand a fetched document to SwiftSoup; at most the text of one matched tag. Collect raw `href`s during the scan and resolve them by hand once it has ended, when the base is known.
- Source rows get `.deleteDisabled(true)` and `.moveDisabled(true)`. Do not collapse categories when edit mode starts: that removes the edit controls of every category but the first.
- The overall limit of the candidate phase is injectable, like RD04's deadline, so its test runs in well under a second; under the fixture transport the app uses a short one. Reaching it ends the unanswered fetches and marks them not checked; the detection call itself is not cancelled and returns.
- The detector keeps at most five parsed feeds in memory, and only until the sheet closes. Release them when the model resets.
- Fixture transport table (all `example.invalid`): a feed; a page declaring two feeds; a page declaring one; a page with a title and no feed; an address answering 403; an unreachable address; an address that times out; one suggested address (BBC World), answered with a synthetic feed; a page declaring one feed that answers and one that does not answer within the short candidate limit; and one address that answers a feed, then fails, then answers again, counted per launch, for criterion 13. Anything else is unreachable.
- `Category` needs RD03's alias in the app. No source folder named `Data`, `Build` or `Dist`. Nothing newer than iOS 26 API and Swift 6.2 syntax.
- The mock-up's amber warning and green tick are not palette tokens. Use the text tokens; the glyph carries the state.
- The release-configuration build must succeed with every debug seam compiled out; that the seams are all inside `#if DEBUG` is a review check.
